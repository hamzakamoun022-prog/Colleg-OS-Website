01 / LEGACY

Born on
hardwood

In 1982, designer Bruce Kilgore put a full-length Nike Air unit into a basketball shoe for the first time. The Air Force 1 — named after the presidential aircraft — landed on NBA courts under the “Original Six”: Moses Malone, Michael Cooper, Bobby Jones, Calvin Natt, Mychal Thompson and Jamaal Wilkes.

Discontinued in 1984, it was resurrected two years later by demand from Baltimore sneaker shops — one of the first retros in history. The court shoe became a street uniform, and the street never gave it back.

02 / ANATOMY

Built like
a monument

The AF-1 doesn’t whisper. A chunky cupsole with concentric-circle pivot outsole, full-grain leather upper in stacked panels, a proud toe box and the Swoosh riding the midfoot like a signature.

Every proportion is deliberate: heavy where it should feel planted, clean where it should stay timeless. It’s the reason a 40-year-old design still looks current out of the box.

03 / DETAILS

The parts that made it immortal

Three decisions in 1982 that still define sneaker design.

A

Nike Air Cushioning

The first basketball shoe with encapsulated Air. The unit is invisible — no window, no logo on the bag — pure function hidden in the midsole.

B

Pivot-Point Outsole

Concentric circles under the forefoot let players rotate without tearing up their knees — and gave the sole its unmistakable footprint.

C

Proclip Ankle Strap

The original high-top shipped with a removable ankle strap — a medical-grade stability idea that became a styling statement.
